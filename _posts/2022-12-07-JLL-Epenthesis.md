In September 2021, Stony Brook University (NY) hosted a [conference devoted to epenthesis](https://www.stonybrook.edu/commcms/epenthesis/program.php).

Renato Oniga and I participated presenting a paper on [this phenomenon in Latin](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1HZEuHTsMBpONp-IB7gHi__x3KXzU11gR/view).

Starting with the concise treatment of this subject provided by Max Niedermann in his [Précis de phonétique historique du Latin](https://archive.org/details/prcisdephont00nied), we first examined how ancient grammarians explained the phenomenon, and then we investigated those linguistic evidences that may explain why Latin speakers resorted to epenthesis in specific phonetic contexts.

The results of our research were subsequently published in the prestigious [Journal of Latin Linguistics](https://dx.doi.org/10.1515/joll-2022-2015).
