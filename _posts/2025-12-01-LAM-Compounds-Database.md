During the [23rd International Colloquium of Latin Linguistics](https://icll2025.uniud.it/) , Andrea Brunello, Giovanni Torresin, and I had the great pleasure of presenting to the scholarly community the final version of the database containing the [linguistic material](https://github.com/AI4CH-UniUD/Genus-Compositicium/tree/main/Dataset) that Renato Oniga and I had collected over more than a decade of research.

Thanks to the invaluable expertise and contributions of Andrea Brunello and Giovanni Torresin, we developed a [Neo4j graph database](http://158.110.146.222:7475/browser/) that supports complex [Cypher queries ](https://github.com/AI4CH-UniUD/Genus-Compositicium/blob/main/Cypher_queries.md), enabling the extraction of a substantial amount of linguistic data concerning the typology and frequency of nominal compounds.
These data can be employed not only to investigate the language of Latin authors but also to explore potential correlations between literary genre and lexical choices.

The latest issue of the journal [Lingue Antiche e Moderne](https://doi.org/10.4424/lam142025-1) contains an article outlining both the theoretical foundations of the research and the practical implementation of the database.
