My name is Alessandro Re: I am a classical philologist whose main research interests focus on Latin linguistics and literature, with particular emphasis on works dating from the 1st century AD to Late Antiquity.

During my doctoral studies at the [University of Udine](https://www.uniud.it/en/uniud-international?set_language=en), under the supervision of Renato Oniga, I focused in particular on Latin nominal compounding and its relationship with the literary genres practised by authors. The outcome of this research was my first monograph, entitled "Genus compositicium: la composizione nominale latina", published in 2020 in the prestigious "Innsbrucker Beiträge zur Sprachwissenschaft" series (n. 165).

In the following years, I obtained my qualification to teach in upper secondary schools and became a tenured teacher in 2018.

Between 2020 and 2024, I held four research fellowships, during which I was able to develop several lines of research that had originated during my doctoral studies. On the one hand, I completed the database of Latin nominal compounds, which incorporates not only the material I had studied myself but also material collected by Renato Oniga in the late 1980s. On the other, I further investigated both linguistic topics – primarily the development of nominal compounding from Latin to the Romance languages, especially Friulian, and the transformation of verb typology from verb-framed in Latin to satellite-framed in Romance, with particular attention once again to Friulian – and more specifically literary questions concerning Pliny the Younger’s "Panegyricus", the satires of Persius and Juvenal, Neronian and Flavian epic poetry, and the implications of Natural Language Processing for the study of intertexts. The latter line of research was pursued through my participation in the [AI4CH-UniUd](https://ai4ch.uniud.it/) research group, "Artificial Intelligence for Cultural Heritage – Interdepartmental Research Center @ University of Udine", in close collaboration with Marco Fucecchi and Andrea Brunello.

Other subjects that have attracted my scholarly interest include glossographical production and its implications for the history of Latin language teaching.

Last but by not least are my studies in liturgy and music, with particular reference to the Ambrosian and Roman traditions. As the study of texts, philology applied to liturgical and musical sources is of fundamental importance for gaining an increasingly clear understanding of the complex layers of development that characterize the various rites of the Western and Eastern Churches.

Clear traces of all these diverse research interests can be found throughout my [publications]({{ '/publications/' | relative_url }}).
